# Frascati Manual 2015: Verified Reference Ledger

## Scope and source boundary

Frascati Manual 2015 is a statistical manual for identifying and measuring R&D activities. Its Chapter 2 explicitly says that its focus is definitions for measurement purposes. It is not a Master's-thesis handbook.  
**Frascati basis:** Chapter 2, §2.4, p. 46.

The locator pages below are **PDF page numbers** in the official OECD PDF (`9789264239012-en.pdf`, 402 pp.), which is the numbering used by the `<!-- Page N -->` markers in the local conversion. To convert to the printed folio shown at the foot of each page, subtract 2 (printed folio = PDF page − 2). Verify any claim beyond this ledger against the official manual before citing it.

## Definition and criteria

| Claim | Verified basis |
|---|---|
| R&D comprises creative and systematic work to increase the stock of knowledge and devise new applications of available knowledge. | Chapter 2, §2.5, p. 46. |
| R&D is aimed at new findings, based on original concepts/hypotheses, uncertain in final outcome or resources, planned/budgeted, and aimed at transferable or tradeable results. | Chapter 2, §2.6, p. 47. |
| The five criteria are novel, creative, uncertain, systematic, and transferable and/or reproducible. | Chapter 2, §2.7, p. 47; the detailed test begins at §2.13, p. 48. |
| All five criteria are to be met, at least in principle. | Chapter 2, §2.8, p. 47; §2.13, p. 48. |
| An R&D activity is deliberate action to generate new knowledge; R&D projects group such activities around a purpose, objectives, and expected outcomes. | Chapter 2, §2.12, p. 48. |
| Novelty excludes copying, imitation, and reverse engineering that does not yield novel knowledge. | Chapter 2, §2.15, p. 48. |
| The measurement focus is new knowledge, not merely a new or improved product/process that applies it. | Chapter 2, §2.16, p. 48. |
| Routine changes are excluded; new methods for common tasks can qualify when the other criteria are met. | Chapter 2, §2.17, p. 49. |
| At the outset, outcome, cost, and/or time cannot be precisely determined; failure to achieve the intended result is possible. | Chapter 2, §2.18, p. 49. |
| Systematic means planned work with records of process and outcome. | Chapter 2, §2.19, p. 49. |
| Results should potentially transfer new knowledge and permit reproduction, including informative negative results; knowledge should be recorded/codified. | Chapter 2, §2.20, p. 50. |

## Types of R&D

| Type | Verified basis |
|---|---|
| Basic research: experimental/theoretical work to acquire new knowledge of underlying foundations, without a particular application or use in view. | Chapter 2, §2.25, p. 52. |
| Applied research: original investigation to acquire new knowledge, primarily toward a specific practical aim/objective. | Chapter 2, §2.29, p. 53. |
| Experimental development: systematic work drawing on research/practical knowledge and producing additional knowledge for new/improved products or processes. | Chapter 2, §2.32, p. 53. |
| Experimental development is not synonymous with overall product development; it ends when the R&D criteria cease to apply. | Chapter 2, §2.34, pp. 53-54. |

## Boundary guidance relevant to Informatics

| Claim | Verified basis |
|---|---|
| Adaptation/customisation using available knowledge without attempting to expand the state of the art should be excluded. | Chapter 2, Table 2.1 commentary, p. 51. |
| Innovation includes activities such as acquisition, training, marketing, design, and software development; these do not automatically meet R&D criteria. | Chapter 2, §§2.46-2.47, p. 62. |
| A prototype can be R&D while its testing is part of an R&D project and supplies essential evidence; copied units after successful testing are not R&D. | Chapter 2, §§2.49-2.50 and Table 2.3, pp. 62-63. |
| Beyond the five core criteria, indicators that may help identify R&D in service activities include links with public research laboratories, the involvement of staff with doctoral degrees or doctoral students, and the publication of research findings in scientific journals or conferences. | Chapter 2, §2.85, p. 71. |

## Prohibited attributions

Do not attribute any of these statements to Frascati:

- “Every Master's thesis must be R&D.”
- “A Master's thesis must create a new algorithm.”
- “A framework, application, AI model, prototype, or publication is automatically R&D.”
- “Frascati prescribes a thesis chapter structure, research-question count, benchmark set, or p-value threshold.”
- “Research master status alone proves the five criteria.”

These may be institutional requirements or AI/S2-Informatics advice, but they are not claims established by the cited Frascati passages.
